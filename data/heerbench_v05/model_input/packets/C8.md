## 1. Purpose

This document compiles the relevant strategic-guidance excerpts that condition the JFC’s Mission Analysis for Operation STEADFAST MARINER. The excerpts are synthetic but are constructed to be consistent with publicly available 2022 NSS / 2022 NDS / 2024 US Strategy Toward Sub-Saharan Africa structure and language patterns. ## 2. National Security Strategy — Sub-Saharan Africa Excerpts (Synthetic, February 2027)

### 2.1 Overarching National Interests

The United States seeks an Africa that is open, democratic, prosperous, and free of malign influence. Sub-Saharan Africa is home to 1.2 billion people, more than half of whom will be under 25 by 2030. The region accounts for a growing share of global energy production (notably from Nigeria, Angola, Equatorial Guinea, and the Gulf of Guinea offshore basin) and is increasingly central to global supply chains for strategic minerals.

The United States maintains active diplomatic and security relationships with all 16 coastal and littoral states of West and Central Africa. Six of those states are signatories to the Yaoundé Code of Conduct (June 2013) regional maritime security architecture: Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, the Republic of Congo, and Angola. The Yaoundé Architecture is the principal multilateral mechanism through which African coastal states coordinate counter-piracy, counter-IUU fishing, and broader maritime security activity.

### 2.2 Strategic Priorities — Maritime Security in the Gulf of Guinea

In the Gulf of Guinea, the United States supports the Yaoundé Architecture and the African Union’s 2050 Africa’s Integrated Maritime Strategy (AIM 2050). The Gulf of Guinea remains the world’s most active piracy theater following the post-2012 suppression of the Horn of Africa piracy peak. Although coordinated international action — including EU NAVFOR–style coordinated maritime presence, French Mission CORYMBE, Spanish Operación PROSPERIDAD, Italian Operazione GABINIA, and the US Maritime Capacity-Building program — has reduced incidents from the 2018–2020 peak, the threat is controlled rather than eliminated.

The December 2026 drawdown of EU Coordinated Maritime Presence (CMP) contributions following budgetary reprioritization toward Eastern Flank deterrence has reduced coalition patrol density in the Gulf of Guinea by an estimated 35%. The post-drawdown coverage gap is concentrated in the Nigerian and Cameroonian EEZs — historically the highest-threat zones for kidnap-for-ransom (K&R) operations against merchant tankers. The 09 February 2027 coordinated attacks against three merchant tankers represent the largest single-day pirate event in the Gulf of Guinea since June 2020.

### 2.3 Strategic Priorities — US Energy Diversification and Atlantic-Basin Supply Chains

The United States imports approximately 10 percent of its crude oil from West African producers (primarily Nigeria, Equatorial Guinea, Angola), valued at approximately \$60 billion annually. West African crude provides supply diversification away from Persian Gulf and Russian sources and is particularly important for US Gulf Coast refiners that are configured for the light-sweet crudes typical of the West African offshore basin. Sustained Gulf of Guinea piracy raises maritime insurance rates, disrupts FPSO and oil-platform operations, and degrades the predictability of West African crude supply.

### 2.4 Strategic Priorities — Sub-Saharan African Partner Development

The 2024 US Strategy Toward Sub-Saharan Africa emphasizes African-led, African-owned security architectures over US-led intervention. The Yaoundé Architecture is the test case for that approach in the maritime domain. US engagement is structured to *enable* African coastal-state navies and law-enforcement organizations to conduct counter-piracy in their own EEZs, with US and European partner forces providing presence, capacity-building, intelligence support, and (where requested) direct kinetic support. The Operation STEADFAST MARINER concept must preserve the Yaoundé architecture’s primacy.

### 2.5 Integration of Instruments of National Power (DIME)

- **Diplomatic.** Department of State has activated bilateral consultations with Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, and São Tomé. AU and ECOWAS notifications have been delivered. Coordination with EU External Action Service ongoing for European coalition contributions. UN Security Council briefing has occurred but no UNSCR is being sought — counter-piracy authority under UNCLOS is settled.

- **Information.** Strategic Communications coordinated through the US Embassy Abuja public affairs apparatus and the AFRICOM Strategic Communications cell. Narrative emphasizes US support for African-led maritime security; US is supporting role, not lead. Counter-narrative against possible pirate-network messaging via social media is being prepared.

- **Military.** USAFRICOM has been directed to plan and prepare to conduct multinational maritime security operations to (a) restore coalition patrol density, (b) recover hostages where feasible in coordination with host-nation forces, and (c) re-establish deterrent presence in the Gulf of Guinea while host-nation partners exercise primary law-enforcement jurisdiction. The President has authorized force-flow to position for execution.

- **Economic.** Treasury / OFAC consideration of targeted sanctions on identified pirate network financial enablers is underway but not yet announced. No broader West Africa economic measures contemplated.

## 3. National Defense Strategy — DoW-Specific Excerpts (Synthetic)

### 3.1 Pacing Challenge and Acute Threats

The 2022 NDS designates the People’s Republic of China as the pacing challenge for the Department of War, and the Russian Federation as an acute threat. The 2024 revision reaffirms both designations. Africa is identified as a region of increasing strategic competition — primarily through PRC and Russian malign activity (PRC infrastructure and basing footprint; Russian Wagner-descended private military presence in Sahel and Central Africa) — but not as a region of direct great-power conventional confrontation.

The 2024 NDS revision identifies maritime security in the African littorals as an enabling capability for the broader Africa Strategy, and explicitly cites the Yaoundé Architecture as a model for partner-led security architectures DoW is directed to support.

### 3.2 Force Allocation Priorities

Per the 2024 NDS implementation guidance:

1.  **First priority of effort:** Defending the homeland (USNORTHCOM, USSPACECOM, USCYBERCOM).

2.  **Second priority:** Deterring strategic attacks against the United States, allies, and partners (USSTRATCOM, regional CCMDs).

3.  **Third priority:** Deterring aggression while being prepared to prevail in conflict in the Indo-Pacific, with the Korean Peninsula and the Taiwan Strait as the two principal contingency drivers.

4.  **Fourth priority:** Deterring opportunistic aggression in Europe (USEUCOM, post-2022 elevated demand).

5.  **Concurrent:** Maintaining presence and partner-development capacity in CENTCOM, AFRICOM, and SOUTHCOM with rotating force contributions.

USAFRICOM is currently the fifth-priority CCMD for force allocation. Operation STEADFAST MARINER will be executed within rotating-contributions framework; no standing dedicated USAFRICOM major surface combatant force is apportioned.

### 3.3 NDS Direction Relevant to STEADFAST MARINER

- Maintain credible partner-development capacity in African littorals through rotating maritime presence.

- Operate as enabling element of African-led architectures (Yaoundé, ECOWAS, ECCAS) rather than as standalone US lead.

- Build interoperability with European partners through standing maritime security exercises (OBANGAME EXPRESS, GRAND AFRICAN NEMO, MULTINATIONAL MARITIME EXERCISE).

- Maintain ability to recover US persons in distress overseas without precommitting to direct kinetic engagement.

- Manage relationship with host nations to preserve future basing access and partnership equities.

## C8:D02 — Regional Intel Summary

# Regional Intelligence Summary — Operation STEADFAST MARINER

**Product Type:** Higher Headquarters Intelligence Background Paper **Date Range Covered:** January 2018 – February 2027 **Source Basis:** OSINT only — IMB Piracy Reporting Centre annual reports 2018–2027, Stable Seas Gulf of Guinea Maritime Security publications, RAND counter-piracy studies, Yaoundé Code of Conduct ICC publications, NAVAF intelligence support detachment products **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E) **Cutoff:** 09 February 2027, 0600Z

## 1. Executive Summary

The Gulf of Guinea piracy threat has been controlled rather than eliminated since the 2018–2020 peak. Coordinated international maritime presence — EU Coordinated Maritime Presence (CMP), French Mission CORYMBE, Spanish Operación PROSPERIDAD, Italian Operazione GABINIA, UK Royal Navy Gulf of Guinea task element, US AMLEP capacity-building — combined with improved Nigerian Navy and Yaoundé Architecture coordination, drove a 70% reduction in attempted attacks from 2020 through 2025. The December 2026 EU CMP drawdown reduced coalition patrol density by approximately 35%. The 09 February 2027 coordinated attacks against three merchant tankers represent the largest single-day pirate event since June 2020 and a clear shift toward coordinated, higher-value targeting.

The primary adversary network is assessed as a Niger Delta-origin kidnap-for-ransom (K&R) organization with operational lineage traceable to the post-MEND (Movement for the Emancipation of the Niger Delta) criminal milieu. Current-generation operations have shifted from oil-bunkering and cargo theft to coordinated K&R against merchant tanker crews. The network is composed of an estimated 4–6 active cells operating from coastal hides in the Niger Delta swamps, with on-shore logistical support from corrupt local officials and small-scale criminal networks. The network’s intent is assessed with low-to-moderate confidence; capabilities are well-characterized.

The principal coalition planning challenges are: (a) rapid rebalancing of patrol density to cover the post-EU CMP coverage gap, (b) recovery of 47 hostages including 4 US persons across three captured vessels with one vessel currently unlocated, (c) preservation of Yaoundé Architecture primacy in coastal-state EEZs while supporting host-nation forces, (d) management of pirate intent ambiguity — whether the 09 February event represents a one-off coordinated K&R or the opening of a sustained campaign.

## 2. Threat Pattern of Life — Gulf of Guinea Piracy, 2018–2027

### 2.1 Threat Evolution

| Period | Characteristic activity | Key drivers |
| 2018–2020 (peak) | Coordinated K&R operations at scale; 130+ kidnapping incidents in 2020 alone; targets included tankers, supply vessels, fishing trawlers | Niger Delta militancy spillover; weak Nigerian Navy patrol coverage; high crude prices enabling ransom demand growth |
| 2021–2022 (initial reduction) | Quarterly incident counts cut by ~40%; some shift toward smaller fishing-vessel targets | Initial deployment of EU CMP, expanded Mission CORYMBE rotations, MOWCA coordination improvements |

| 2025 (controlled) | Incident counts stable at low level; few successful K&R; trend toward opportunistic small-vessel targeting | Sustained coalition presence; improved Nigerian Navy capacity; some pirate-network displacement to Sahel hawala economy |

| **February 2027 (current)** | **09 February 2027 coordinated attacks against three merchant tankers; 47 hostages; one vessel unlocated** | **Assessed exploitation of post-drawdown patrol gap; shift toward coordinated higher-value targeting** |

### 2.2 The 09 February 2027 Event in Detail

At approximately 0420Z on 09 February 2027, three pirate skiffs operating in coordination boarded the Liberian-flag product tanker MV *AURORA STAR* (49,000 DWT, en route Lagos→Lomé with refined product cargo) at position 04°20’N 008°12’E in the Cameroon EEZ. Sixteen crew were taken hostage; the vessel was steered southeast toward the Niger Delta. AIS was spoofed approximately 30 minutes after boarding to indicate a position approximately 80 nm to the north-northwest. *AURORA STAR* has not been visually contacted since 0640Z; the assessed actual position is in the network of hides between the Brass River and Forcados River estuaries.

At approximately 0510Z, a second three-skiff team boarded the Maltese-flag product tanker MV *BENEDICT GALLOWAY* (38,000 DWT, en route Port Harcourt→Walvis Bay) at 04°08’N 007°56’E in the Nigerian EEZ. Eighteen crew were taken hostage; ransom contact was established on 09 February 2027 at approximately 1830Z via Nigerian intermediary. Vessel was abandoned by pirates at 1740Z and is now under Nigerian Navy custody at Calabar.

At approximately 0540Z, a third pirate team (assessed at 2 skiffs with 4–5 pirates each) boarded the Marshall Islands-flag chemical tanker MV *KINIPELA HOPE* (32,000 DWT, en route Bonny→Algeciras) at 04°15’N 008°22’E in the Equatoguinean EEZ. Thirteen crew were taken hostage; the vessel was released at 1120Z (under 6 hours after boarding) — atypical brevity, assessed as indicating either insufficient pirate manpower to maintain three simultaneous prizes or deliberate signaling.

The three vessels carried 47 crew total: 16 *AURORA STAR* (4 US persons, 7 Filipino, 5 Liberian); 18 *BENEDICT GALLOWAY* (released — all accounted for); 13 *KINIPELA HOPE* (released — all accounted for). Net hostage situation: 16 hostages aboard the unlocated *AURORA STAR*, including 4 US-flag-state mariners. ICRC notification of the four US persons is pending.

### 2.3 Coordination Indicators

The three attacks display coordination indicators not characteristic of opportunistic Gulf of Guinea piracy:

- Geographic dispersion across three EEZs (NGA, CMR, GEQ) within a 70-minute window argues for coordinated planning.

- AIS-spoofing technique on *AURORA STAR* is consistent across all three attacks (delayed, distance-shifted false position).

- Target selection (all product/chemical tankers, all 30,000–50,000 DWT class) suggests deliberate value-class selection rather than opportunistic interception.

- Brevity of the *KINIPELA HOPE* operation suggests planned exit, not interrupted operation.

### 2.4 Pirate Network Assessment

Assessed adversary: Niger Delta-origin K&R network, lineage to post-MEND criminal milieu (2009 amnesty cohort and their successors). Current generation operates as a loose federation of 4–6 cells, each with 15–25 active personnel, sharing logistics, intelligence, and ransom-negotiation infrastructure but executing independent operations.

Cell-level capability: - **Light infantry weapons.** AK-pattern rifles (mixed-origin, mostly older stocks routed via Sahel arms-trafficking), RPG-7 launchers, light mortars (60mm and 81mm — limited reports), GPMGs mounted on captured speedboats. - **Small-boat operations.** Modified fishing skiffs (12–14 meters, twin-outboard, capable of ~30 knots sustained); captured commercial speedboats and pilot vessels; mother-ship operations from fishing trawlers up to 200 nm offshore have been observed in 2024–2025. - **Communications.** Iridium and Inmarsat satellite phones (multiple per cell), end-to-end-encrypted messaging applications (Signal, Telegram, Wickr observed), VHF maritime radio (channel 16 monitoring + working channels). - **Maritime intelligence.** Commercial AIS-monitoring tools (mostly via public-internet aggregators); on-shore observation from port-area informants; corrupt-official intelligence streams. - **No cyber, space, or strategic capability.** No electronic warfare. No GPS denial. No cyber-influence campaigns. No remote sensing beyond commercial AIS.

Cell coordination is assessed as facilitated by 2–3 senior network figures who control ransom-negotiation infrastructure (typically via Lagos-area intermediaries and hawala networks) and provide top-level target-selection guidance. Senior figures rarely participate in attacks directly. Identities of senior figures are not confirmed in OSINT; one named individual (“ALATA-1,” likely pseudonym) has appeared in IMB reporting since 2023 but is not visually identified.

## 3. Adversary Strategic Posture (Pirate Network)

### 3.1 Most Likely Adversary COA (Most Likely)

The 09 February 2027 event is the opening of a sustained K&R campaign exploiting the post-EU CMP coverage gap. The network will conduct 2–4 additional coordinated attacks over the next 60–90 days, escalating to higher-value targets (15,000+ DWT crude carriers, FPSO support vessels, possibly LNG carriers) if the initial response is judged insufficient. Hostage demands will be staged: lower-value hostage releases in exchange for sub-million-dollar ransoms in the near term, building toward higher-value hostage demand cycles. **Confidence: moderate.** **Indicators:** continued AIS-spoofing reports; intercepted communications suggesting network coordination; observed pre-positioning of skiffs at hide locations.

### 3.2 Most Dangerous Adversary COA

The 09 February 2027 event is a network probing operation to assess coalition response capacity prior to a campaign against higher-value strategic infrastructure — specifically, an attempt to capture an FPSO support vessel or to seize an LNG carrier with the intent of denying the LNG supply chain or extracting a state-level ransom. The network may have, or be developing, contacts with state-actor proxies (assessed possibly Russian Federation-aligned given Sahel arms-trafficking patterns) interested in disrupting Western energy supply chains. **Confidence: low — limited reporting; significant strategic-consequence delta if true.** **Indicators:** evidence of pre-attack reconnaissance against FPSO or LNG infrastructure; weapons or comms upgrades inconsistent with K&R economics; political-messaging in hostage demands beyond ransom.

### 3.3 Alternative Adversary COA — One-Off Event

The 09 February 2027 event represents a one-off coordinated operation by the network to demonstrate continued capability post-coalition drawdown, intended to establish ransom-negotiation leverage rather than open a sustained campaign. After the *AURORA STAR* ransom is settled, network tempo returns to 2025–2026 controlled-baseline levels. **Confidence: moderate.** **Indicators:** rapid ransom-settlement negotiations; absence of further attacks within 30 days; intercepted communications suggesting the network considers the event “complete.”

### 3.4 Adversary Capabilities vs Intent Assessment (Kent Split)

**Capabilities (high confidence):** Pirate network capabilities are well-characterized through 6+ years of IMB reporting and Yaoundé Architecture intelligence sharing. Network can sustain 2–4 major coordinated K&R operations per quarter at current capacity; cannot sustain operations that require sophisticated EW or counter-naval capability; cannot threaten military vessels.

**Intent (low confidence):** Whether the 09 February 2027 event indicates a one-off, a sustained campaign, or a probing operation toward higher-value targets cannot be determined from current reporting. Three COAs (above) are presented with explicit confidence levels. The intent question is the principal J2 ambiguity in this scenario.

## 4. Coastal-State Posture

### 4.1 Nigeria

**Government:** President-elect’s administration (took office 29 May 2027 in scenario timeline; STEADFAST MARINER planning is occurring under outgoing administration). Inherited Yaoundé commitments and Nigerian Navy modernization program. Public commitment to counter-piracy is strong; private friction with US over basing access and SOFA arrangements.

**Nigerian Navy:** Eastern Naval Command at Calabar (RADM-equivalent, area of responsibility includes Bonny–Forcados sector — the primary pirate operating area). Western Naval Command at Lagos. Capability has materially improved since 2020: NNS *PROSPERITY*-class offshore patrol vessels, helicopter detachments, embarked Marines (Nigerian Navy Special Boat Service). Operating tempo is constrained by fuel and parts availability; mission availability of major surface units assessed at 65–70%.

**Posture on STEADFAST MARINER:** Welcomes coalition support; insistent on Nigerian primary jurisdiction in NGA EEZ; sensitive to public optics of foreign naval forces in Nigerian waters.

### 4.2 Cameroon

**Government:** Stable; long-running incumbent. Yaoundé Architecture original signatory.

**Marine Nationale Camerounaise:** Modest fleet (5 patrol vessels operational); coastal-defense role primarily. Hosts MMCC Douala (Yaoundé Architecture Zone D coordination center). MMCC Douala assessed as one of the more capable Yaoundé MMCCs.

**Posture on STEADFAST MARINER:** Strong supportive; offers port access at Douala for coalition operations; will second liaison officers to NAVAF MOC.

**Government:** Long-running incumbent; relationship with US is transactional. Hosts US oil-industry presence (Punta Europa LNG facility).

### 4.4 Gabon, São Tomé and Príncipe

## 5. Coalition Posture

### 5.1 Standing Coalition Presence Pre-09 February 2027

- **US:** AMLEP capacity-building program; rotating SIXTH Fleet detachment for OBANGAME EXPRESS and GRAND AFRICAN NEMO exercises; no standing combatant presence.

- **France:** Mission CORYMBE — continuous since 1990; typically one FREMM-class or La Fayette-class frigate + one support vessel.

- **Spain:** Operación PROSPERIDAD — typically one BAM-class offshore patrol vessel since 2020.

- **Italy:** Operazione GABINIA — typically one PPA-class or FREMM-class frigate since 2020.

- **Portugal:** Periodic NRP frigate deployment (~3 months/year).

- **United Kingdom:** Royal Navy permanent Gulf of Guinea task element; typically OPV (Forth/Spey/Trent class) plus visiting frigate. **Note:** UK presence is independent of (formerly) EU CMP and continues unaffected by the December 2026 drawdown.

- **EU:** EU CMP (Coordinated Maritime Presence) — drawn down December 2026; previously coordinated FRA / ESP / ITA / PRT contributions plus periodic Greek and Romanian rotations.

### 5.2 Post-Drawdown Coverage Gap (December 2026 – Current)

The December 2026 EU CMP drawdown reduced standing coalition combatant presence in the Gulf of Guinea by approximately 35% (loss of periodic Greek, Romanian, and Danish rotations; reduced French operational tempo from continuous to ~75% coverage). UK presence is unaffected. National contributions (FRA / ESP / ITA / PRT) continue under bilateral arrangements but are reduced in tempo.

The Niger Delta operating area (Bonny–Forcados sector) — historically the highest-threat zone — has assessed coalition patrol coverage of approximately 40% of pre-drawdown levels. This coverage gap is the principal driver of the 09 February 2027 event.

### 5.3 Yaoundé Architecture Operational Status

- **ICC Yaoundé** (Inter-Regional Coordination Centre): Functional; provides daily fused operational picture to coastal states and partners; assessed as a coordination hub rather than a tasking authority.

- **MMCC Douala** (Maritime Multinational Coordination Centre, Zone D — Nigerian/Cameroonian/Equatoguinean/Gabonese coastal waters): Assessed as the most capable MMCC; effective at incident response coordination.

- **MMCC Pointe-Noire** (Zone A — Republic of Congo, Angola): Less capable; coordination quality varies.

- **Other MMCCs / coordination cells:** Variable capacity.

Yaoundé Architecture is voluntary, lacks enforcement mechanism, and depends on coastal-state political will. Coordination quality varies materially across zones. This is a designed-in inference point for the MA staff (#6 in 00_README.md).

## 6. Implications for the Operational Approach

The Regional Intelligence Summary supports the following analytic conclusions:

1.  **The 09 February 2027 event is qualitatively different from background Gulf of Guinea piracy.** Coordination across three EEZs, target selection consistency, and AIS-spoofing tradecraft suggest organized network action, not opportunistic incidents.

2.  **The coverage gap is the proximate enabler.** The pirate network is exploiting the post-EU CMP drawdown. Restoring coalition patrol density is necessary but may not be sufficient; sustained presence is required to deny the gap.

3.  **Pirate intent ambiguity is the binding J2 problem.** Three plausible adversary COAs (sustained campaign / probing operation / one-off event) have materially different operational implications. Intent-related CCIRs are the J2 priority.

4.  **Yaoundé primacy is a planning constraint, not a planning option.** US and coalition operations must support, not displace, host-nation primary jurisdiction in EEZ. The CIPG must articulate this constraint and its operational implications.

5.  **Personnel Recovery / Hostage Rescue (PR/HR) is a parallel mission set under PPD-30 with FBI as Lead Federal Agency.** The 4 US persons aboard the unlocated *AURORA STAR* are isolated US persons in non-state captivity. PPD-30 (24 June 2015) governs the US Government response: FBI HRFC is LFA; SPEHA leads diplomatic engagement; the military hostage-rescue option under JP 3-50 is the option of last resort with execution authority retained at the NCA. The MA staff must reason about (a) supported/supporting relationships between NAVAF (maritime security) and SOCAFRICA’s JSOTF–STEADFAST MARINER (PR/HR), (b) triggering conditions for shifting from FBI-led negotiation to military rescue, and (c) host-nation jurisdiction during any rescue. The standing counter-piracy mission remains the operation’s center of gravity; PR/HR is a parallel mission set, not a branch on it.

6.  **Coalition rebalancing is the J5 priority.** Allocation of finite UK / FRA / ESP / ITA / PRT / US patrol assets across coverage zones, plus the question of standing presence vs reactive surge, is the J5 planning load. Designed-in inference point \#1 (coalition rebalancing) is the principal J5 ambiguity in this scenario.

The adversary is a **decentralized criminal network**, not a state-aligned organization. There is no formal C2 structure analogous to a military OOB. The network is best modeled as a loose federation with three layers:

## 4. Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs)

### 4.1 Target selection

- Preferred targets: product tankers, chemical tankers, crude carriers in the 30,000–80,000 DWT class

- Target intelligence developed from AIS monitoring + port-area observation; targets typically selected 24–72 hours pre-attack

- Higher-value target ambitions noted but not consistently executed: FPSO support vessels, LNG carriers, oil-platform supply boats

### 4.2 Approach and boarding

- Skiff approach typically from quarter or bow; uses sun angle and sea-state to reduce visual signature

- Boarding via grappling hooks and ladders; takes 3–8 minutes from initial approach to control of bridge

- Crew typically corralled to bridge or messdeck; non-essential personnel restrained

- Master and chief engineer separated (high-value individuals for ransom negotiation)

### 4.3 Post-boarding

- AIS spoofing initiated within 30 minutes of boarding (delayed and distance-shifted false positions)

- Course altered toward Niger Delta hide network; transit times typically 12–36 hours depending on initial position

- Hostages transferred from vessel to coastal hide within 6–24 hours of arrival at hide network

- Vessel either retained for use (rare) or released after ransom negotiation (common)

### 4.4 Ransom negotiation

- Initial contact typically 24–72 hours post-event via Nigerian intermediary

- Negotiation cycle typically 30–90 days

- Recent ransom levels (2024–2026): US persons \$1.5–3M each; OECD-state mariners \$0.5–1.5M each; non-OECD mariners \$200K–800K each

- Payment via hawala or cryptocurrency (increasing trend); intermediaries take 10–20% of total

- Hostage release typically within 24–72 hours of payment confirmation

### 4.5 Reconstitution cycle

After major captures by Nigerian Navy or coalition operations, network cells typically reconstitute in 6–18 months, drawing on Niger Delta unemployed youth labor pool and pre-existing arms/equipment caches.

## C8:D06 — Critical Geography

# Critical Geography — Operation STEADFAST MARINER

**Product Type:** Operational Environment Description — Geography, Climatology, Maritime Domain **Scope:** Gulf of Guinea (Bonny–Forcados–Cameroon EEZ–Equatorial Guinea EEZ–Gabon EEZ–São Tomé) **Source basis:** OSINT — NIMA / NGA Sailing Directions, IMO MSC publications, NOAA West Africa Atlas, AGRHYMET West Africa Climate Outlook, IMB Piracy Reporting Centre geographic notes **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E)

## 1. Theater Overview

The Gulf of Guinea is the northeastern lobe of the Atlantic Ocean, bounded by the West African coast from approximately 7°W (Cape Palmas, Liberia) to approximately 14°S (Cabinda, Angola). For STEADFAST MARINER purposes, the operating area is the central Gulf — approximately 2°S to 6°N, 4°E to 12°E — covering the EEZs of Nigeria, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, and São Tomé and Príncipe.

**Distances (relevant):** - Lagos to Calabar: ~470 nm (Nigerian coastal transit) - Calabar to Douala: ~120 nm - Bonny to Punta Europa (Bioko): ~145 nm - Bonny to São Tomé: ~340 nm - Forcados to Bonny: ~85 nm - MMCC Douala to MMCC Pointe-Noire: ~510 nm - NAVAF MOC Naples to operating area (great circle): ~3,200 nm

**Bathymetry:** Continental shelf is narrow in the central Gulf; significant depths (\>200m) are reached within 30–80 nm of shore in most sectors. Niger Delta region has extensive shallow-water (\<20m) approaches and complex bar-mouth navigation. Bioko Island sits on its own continental shelf with deep water approaches. São Tomé and Príncipe are isolated oceanic islands with deepwater approaches.

## 2. Operating Sub-Sectors

For STEADFAST MARINER, the operating area is divided into four planning sub-sectors:

### 2.1 Sector A — Niger Delta Approaches (Bonny–Forcados)

**Geography:** Niger Delta is the largest river delta in Africa (~70,000 km²), formed by the Niger River and its distributaries. Coast is low-lying; mangrove swamps extend ~30 nm inland in places. Coastal water visibility frequently degraded by silt outflow during wet season (May–October).

**Pirate operating environment:** Primary pirate operating area. Coastal hide network is extensive in the swamps between the Brass and Forcados rivers; estimated 40+ potential hide sites for skiffs and captured vessels. Hide locations can be identified only with HUMINT or sustained ISR; satellite imagery alone is insufficient due to vegetation cover.

**Maritime activity:** Heavy commercial tanker traffic to Bonny LNG and Forcados crude terminals. Approximately 8–14 tanker movements per day in this sector.

**NAI / TAI relevance:** Highest density of NAIs and TAIs (see 15_Event_Template_NAI_Matrix.xlsx); Niger Delta sector is the operational center of gravity for pirate-network operations.

### 2.2 Sector B — Cameroon EEZ + Bioko Approaches

**Geography:** Continental shelf extends ~30–50 nm offshore; deeper water beyond. Bioko Island rises sharply from the deep Atlantic basin; northern Bioko has Pico Basile (3,012m) visible from offshore in clear weather.

**Pirate operating environment:** Secondary pirate operating area; transit zone between Niger Delta hide network and Cameroonian/Equatoguinean EEZ targets. MV *AURORA STAR* boarded in this sector.

**Maritime activity:** LNG traffic to/from Punta Europa; commercial tanker traffic; some fishing-fleet activity.

### 2.3 Sector C — Gabon EEZ + São Tomé approach

**Geography:** Deeper water; less complex coastal geography. São Tomé harbor is a natural deepwater anchorage (depths to ~30m within 1 nm of shore).

**Pirate operating environment:** Lower threat; transit-only for pirate operations. Some mother-ship-supported operations have been observed reaching this sector but are rare.

**Maritime activity:** Tanker traffic to/from Gabon (Port-Gentil, Libreville) and onward routing toward southern Africa.

### 2.4 Sector D — Open-Ocean Transit (300+ nm offshore)

**Geography:** Open ocean; no fixed infrastructure; sea-state-dependent operating environment.

**Pirate operating environment:** Mother-ship-supported pirate operations have reached this sector in 2024–2025. Lower threat than coastal sectors but not zero.

**Maritime activity:** Through-traffic to/from Cape Town routing.

## 3. Chokepoints and Sea Lanes

The Gulf of Guinea does not contain a single dominant chokepoint analogous to Hormuz or Malacca. However, several operational chokepoints concentrate maritime activity:

- **Bonny Channel approach** — concentrated tanker traffic to Bonny LNG and crude terminals.

- **Forcados approach** — concentrated crude tanker traffic.

- **Lagos approach (Apapa/Lekki)** — concentrated container traffic.

- **Cape Lopez approach (Gabon)** — concentrated crude tanker traffic for Gabonese exports.

- **Inter-island channels (Bioko–Mainland; São Tomé–Príncipe)** — concentrated short-haul shipping.

Coalition patrol planning frequently uses chokepoint-based station-keeping to maximize coverage of high-value traffic.

## 4. Climatology

### 4.1 Seasonal pattern

The Gulf of Guinea has a tropical monsoonal climate with two distinct seasons:

- **Wet season (April–October):** Heavy precipitation, peaked July–September. Reduced visibility from rain and fog. Sea state generally moderate (2–4 m significant wave height). Reduced helicopter operating windows.

- **Dry season (November–March, “Harmattan”):** Reduced precipitation. Sahel-origin dust haze from December–February degrades visibility (typically 2–5 nm horizontal). Sea state generally calm to moderate (1–3 m).

**February 2027 (operation window):** Late Harmattan. Expected visibility 3–6 nm with periodic dust-haze degradation. Sea state 1.5–2.5 m. Air temperatures 26–30°C. Sea surface temperature ~27°C.

### 4.2 Operational implications

- **Visual ISR degraded by Harmattan dust** in February; thermal sensors recommended.

- **Helicopter operations** generally feasible; constraint is hot/high performance limits in the afternoon.

- **VBSS operations** sea-state limit of 2.5 m significant wave height is at margin in February.

- **Small-boat operations** by pirate skiffs are not constrained by Harmattan conditions — pirates operate effectively in degraded visibility.

## 5. Electromagnetic Spectrum and Communications Environment

### 5.1 Baseline EMS

The Gulf of Guinea EMS is **uncontested** in the state-actor sense. Pirate networks do not employ EW or GPS denial. Some commercial RFI from oil-platform operations (especially Punta Europa LNG) creates localized noise floors in HF and certain UHF bands.

### 5.2 AIS environment

Commercial AIS coverage in the Gulf of Guinea is moderate — better coastal (within 30 nm) than open-ocean. Public AIS aggregators (MarineTraffic, FleetMon, VesselFinder) provide comprehensive coverage of compliant vessels. Pirate AIS-spoofing tradecraft (transmitting false position from captured vessel after hijacking) is well-established; the 09 February 2027 event used this tradecraft on *AURORA STAR*.

### 5.3 Satellite communications

Iridium and Inmarsat satellite phone coverage is comprehensive across the operating area. Commercial broadband (VSAT) is available aboard most modern merchant tankers and coalition warships. SATCOM is not contested.

### 5.4 Cyber attribution challenges

Cyber attribution against pirate-network communications is limited primarily by encrypted-messaging applications (Signal, Telegram, Wickr). HUMINT-driven access to pirate-network smartphones has been the primary attribution mechanism in recent Nigerian Navy operations.

### 1. SITUATION

#### (a) General

On 09 February 2027, between 0420Z and 0540Z, three pirate elements assessed as a single Niger Delta-origin kidnap-for-ransom (K&R) network executed coordinated attacks against three merchant tankers in the Cameroonian, Nigerian, and Equatoguinean Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZs) of the Gulf of Guinea. Forty-seven (47) merchant mariners were taken hostage across the three vessels, including four (4) US-flag-state mariners aboard the Liberian-flagged product tanker MV *AURORA STAR* (49,000 DWT). Two of the three vessels (MV *BENEDICT GALLOWAY*, MV *KINIPELA HOPE*) were released the same day with their 31 crew accounted for. MV *AURORA STAR* has not been visually contacted since 0640Z; the four (4) US persons are assessed as held in the Niger Delta coastal-hide network and remain in pirate custody.

This event is the largest single-day Gulf of Guinea pirate incident since June 2020. Network targeting consistency, AIS-spoofing tradecraft, and the 70-minute geographic dispersion across three EEZs indicate coordinated action exploiting the December 2026 EU Coordinated Maritime Presence (CMP) drawdown, which reduced coalition Gulf of Guinea patrol density by approximately thirty-five percent (35%).

The President has directed, and the Secretary of War has approved, military action to (i) restore coalition maritime patrol density in the Gulf of Guinea, (ii) support the FBI-led recovery of the four US persons consistent with reference (b), and (iii) deter further pirate-network action against international shipping in coordination with host-nation and coalition partners.

Adversary capabilities are well-characterized; intent is murkier — three plausible adversary courses of action (sustained K&R campaign, probing toward higher-value targets including FPSO and LNG carriers, one-off coordinated event) are carried in the AFRICOM JIPOE.

## Annex A — Operational Law (OPLAW)

### A.1 Legal basis for counter-piracy operations

Counter-piracy operations under STEADFAST MARINER are conducted under well-established international and domestic legal frameworks:

- **UNCLOS Articles 100–107** (Treaty Doc. 103-39; entered into force 1994; US is signatory but not party — applies as customary international law). Establishes universal jurisdiction over piracy on the high seas; defines piracy; establishes right of all states to seize pirate vessels and arrest persons and seize property on board.

- **SUA Convention** (1988 Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts Against the Safety of Maritime Navigation) — applicable to non-piracy maritime crimes (e.g., maritime terrorism).

- **Yaoundé Code of Conduct** (June 2013) — voluntary regional architecture for maritime security cooperation in West and Central Africa.

- **Coastal-state domestic law** — applies in territorial sea (≤12 nm) and contiguous zone (≤24 nm) absolutely; applies in EEZ (≤200 nm) for limited purposes (fisheries, customs, environment).

- **Bilateral SOFAs** — US has bilateral SOFA arrangements with each Yaoundé signatory; specifics vary by country.

### A.2 Jurisdictional framework

|:---|----|----|----|
| Maritime zone | Distance from coast | Jurisdiction | Applicable to STEADFAST MARINER |

### A.3 Personnel Recovery / Hostage Rescue Authority

The four US-flag-state mariners aboard MV *AURORA STAR* are isolated US persons in non-state captivity — they are hostages held by a non-state criminal/terror network on foreign soil. **The correct doctrinal framing is Personnel Recovery / Hostage Rescue (PR/HR) under JP 3-50, NOT Noncombatant Evacuation Operation (NEO) under JP 3-68.** NEO doctrine governs the State Department-led evacuation of designated noncombatants from a host nation in crisis (e.g., civil unrest, war, natural disaster) — it does not govern the recovery of US persons held hostage by a non-state network.

Authority to conduct US-Government-coordinated hostage recovery derives from:

- **PPD-30**, *US Nationals Taken Hostage Abroad and Personnel Recovery Efforts* (24 June 2015). Establishes the FBI Hostage Recovery Fusion Cell (HRFC) as the Lead Federal Agency for US hostages held overseas; establishes the Hostage Response Group (HRG) at the NSC; establishes the Special Presidential Envoy for Hostage Affairs (SPEHA) at the Department of State.

- **Joint Publication 3-50** (Personnel Recovery). Governs DoW military Personnel Recovery doctrine for isolated personnel (IP), including hostage rescue. Establishes the PR architecture (JPRC at the JTF level, PRCC at the component level), ISOPREP and PRMR reporting framework, and the options-of-last-resort framework for military rescue.

- **18 U.S.C. § 1651** (Piracy under law of nations). US criminal jurisdiction over piracy on the high seas.

- **10 U.S.C. § 164** (Combatant commander authorities). CDR USAFRICOM authority to plan and execute military operations within the AOR.

- **22 U.S.C. § 2671** (State Department emergency expenditures). DoS authority for emergency expenditures related to US citizens overseas.

**Execution authority for the military hostage-rescue option (the “rescue of last resort”) is RETAINED at the National Command Authority** (Presidential decision required, recommended by SecWar and CJCS through the NSC Hostage Response Group process under PPD-30). Planning, force preparation, rehearsal, and positioning authority is delegated to CDR USAFRICOM via the CJCS EXORD. Coordination with the Government of Nigeria via DAO Lagos, US Embassy Abuja, and SPEHA is mandatory before any rescue execution involving Nigerian territory or airspace.

### A.5 Rules of Engagement (ROE) — Summary

The ROE annex (separately distributed at SECRET level) follows standard counter-piracy ROE under UNCLOS Articles 100–107 with hostage-safety primacy. Key features:

- **Standing self-defense** authority retained throughout

## Annex E — Personnel Recovery / Hostage Rescue (PR/HR)

The recovery of the four US-flag-state mariners aboard MV *AURORA STAR* is a **Personnel Recovery / Hostage Rescue (PR/HR)** mission under JP 3-50 — not a NEO under JP 3-68. NEO doctrine governs the State Department-led evacuation of designated noncombatants from a host nation in crisis; it does not govern the recovery of US persons held hostage by a non-state network on foreign soil.

The US Government response is governed by PPD-30 (24 June 2015): FBI Hostage Recovery Fusion Cell (HRFC) is the Lead Federal Agency; the NSC Hostage Response Group (HRG) is the senior policy coordination body; the Special Presidential Envoy for Hostage Affairs (SPEHA) leads diplomatic engagement with the Government of Nigeria and other host nations. - **Phase 1 — Location confirmation.** Theater ISR (P-8, MQ-9B, partner-nation contributions) and HUMINT develop a fixed *AURORA STAR* hide location with confidence sufficient for direct-action planning. JPRC validates the ISOPREP/PRMR picture.

- **Phase 2 — Diplomatic and host-nation coordination.** SPEHA, supported by US Embassy Abuja and DAO Lagos, secures Government of Nigeria coordination for entry into Nigerian territory. The form of coordination (overt host-nation invitation; quiet host-nation acquiescence; or unilateral action under hot-pursuit / self-defense theory) is decided at the NCA level on case-specific facts.

- **Phase 3 — Force insertion (JSOTF-led).** The theater SEAL detachment (planned SEAL Team 4 under JSOTF–STEADFAST MARINER) is the **primary direct-action entry team** for the hostage-rescue mission. The 26th MEU’s Maritime Raid Force (MRF) is the **supporting** force: outer cordon, blocking, extraction security, and CASEVAC. Rotary insertion is from USS *FORT LAUDERDALE* (LPD) using MEU ACE assets (MH-60S, MV-22). AFSOC MC-130J supports extended-range insertion or aerial refueling as required.

# Commander’s Initial Planning Guidance (CIPG) — Operation STEADFAST MARINER

**Issuing Commander:** CDR USAFRICOM **Date Issued:** 10 February 2027 **Issued to:** COMUSNAVAF and supporting components; Combined Maritime Task Force 100 (CTF-100) Commander **Source-Document Layer:** Commander’s Initial Planning Guidance per JP 5-0 **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E)

## 1. Commander’s Initial Intent

### 1.1 Purpose

To restore the maritime security regime in the Gulf of Guinea by re-establishing coalition presence following the December 2026 EU Coordinated Maritime Presence drawdown, while recovering US and partner-nation hostages aboard the unlocated MV *AURORA STAR* and supporting host-nation law-enforcement primacy under the Yaoundé Architecture.

### 1.2 Method

Multinational coalition surface presence integrated under NAVAF tactical control (CMTF/CTF-100), augmented by US forward forces (DDG, LPD, ESB) and theater ISR (P-8, MQ-9B). Direct kinetic action against pirate-network on-shore infrastructure conducted by host-nation forces with coalition enabling support. The military Personnel Recovery / Hostage Rescue (PR/HR) option for the four US-flag-state mariners aboard *AURORA STAR* — led by JSOTF–STEADFAST MARINER under SOCAFRICA — is preserved in a ready-posture and executed only on order from the NCA, with the FBI HRFC LFA-led negotiation track running concurrently under PPD-30 (24 June 2015).

### 1.3 End-state

Three concurrent end-state conditions:

1.  **Coalition patrol density restored to pre-EU CMP drawdown levels** in the Bonny–Forcados–Cameroon EEZ corridor, with sustained 6-month presence commitment from coalition partners (UK, FRA, ESP, ITA, PRT) and standing US AMLEP engagement.

2.  **US and partner-nation hostages aboard MV *AURORA STAR* recovered or accounted for**, with hostage situation closed via either coalition/host-nation recovery operation or via successful ransom negotiation and release.

3.  **Pirate-network operational tempo returned to 2025–2026 controlled-baseline levels**, measured by incident counts in IMB reporting; Yaoundé Architecture coordination relationships preserved and strengthened.

## 2. Operational Design Elements

### 2.1 Strategic Objectives

- Restore maritime security regime in Gulf of Guinea.

- Recover US persons in distress without precipitating broader US military commitment to West African counter-piracy.

- Preserve African-led security architecture (Yaoundé) and US partner-development equities.

- Demonstrate US commitment to coalition partners (UK, FRA, ESP, ITA, PRT) and to commercial-shipping protection.

### 2.2 Termination Criteria (designed-in inference point — partially specified)

The mission terminates when:

- Coalition patrol density is restored AND sustained for 60 days AND coalition partners commit to 6 months of sustained presence; **OR**

- Hostage situation is closed AND pirate-network operational tempo has returned to controlled-baseline levels for 90 days; **OR**

- CDR USAFRICOM determines (with SecWar concurrence) that further operations are not productive given the operational situation.

### 2.5 Decisive Conditions / Lines of Effort

Three Lines of Effort, conducted in parallel:

### 2.7 Operational Approach

The operation employs a **presence-and-pressure** approach rather than a **find-and-fix** approach. Coalition surface presence in the Bonny–Forcados corridor is the principal deterrent; opportunistic VBSS exploits pirate-network exposure during operations; financial-intelligence pressure on Lagos-area intermediaries and hawala flows is the principal disruption mechanism. Hostage recovery is the most-likely Direct Action requirement but is conditional on locating *AURORA STAR* — without location confirmation, the principal hostage-recovery vector is ransom-negotiation support through commercial channels.

### 2.8 Acceptable and Unacceptable Risk

**Acceptable risk:** - Coalition asset wear from sustained tempo - Limited diplomatic friction with host-nation governments over EEZ access requests - Failure to recover *AURORA STAR* hostages via direct action (acceptable if ransom-resolution path is open)

**Unacceptable risk:** - Hostage casualties during attempted rescue (drives no-engagement-that-risks-hostage-casualties posture) - Coalition disengagement during operation (drives consistent partner-burden-sharing) - Yaoundé Architecture damage (drives indirect-approach posture in coastal-state EEZs) - Inadvertent escalation with host-nation military forces (drives coordinated-coordination requirement)

## 3. Hostage-Recovery vs Deterrence Trade-Off (Designed-in inference point \#2)

The CIPG explicitly addresses the hostage-recovery vs deterrence trade-off:

**Aggressive hostage-recovery posture** (priority on JSOTF military PR/HR readiness and direct-action rehearsal; supported/supporting from NAVAF): - Pro: maximum probability of US-person recovery - Pro: demonstrates US commitment to US-mariner protection - Con: pirate-network may relocate or shift operations to harder-to-reach areas - Con: hostage-casualty risk on engagement - Con: may pre-empt ransom-negotiation closure

**Conservative deterrence posture** (priority on coalition presence and ransom-negotiation support): - Pro: lowest hostage-casualty risk - Pro: preserves ransom-resolution path - Pro: builds sustained coalition presence - Con: longer time-to-resolution for hostage situation - Con: family-liaison and political pressure

**CDR USAFRICOM direction:** Posture is **conditional deterrence with prepared aggressive recovery**. Default posture is conservative deterrence (LOE 1 + LOE 3 sustained; LOE 2 limited to ISR development and JSOTF PR/HR ready-posture in support of the FBI HRFC LFA-led negotiation track); aggressive-recovery posture (NCA-authorized military hostage rescue executed by JSOTF–STEADFAST MARINER under JP 3-50) is held in reserve and authorized only by the NCA via a separate CJCS EXORD or Presidential Decision Memorandum following the NSC HRG process under PPD-30. The MA staff must articulate specific transition conditions (what observation would shift posture from conservative to aggressive? what triggers an NSC HRG review?). This is the dominant operational-design tension in the operation.
