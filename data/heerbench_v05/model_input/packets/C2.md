## C2:D01 — Strategic Context

# 01 — STRATEGIC CONTEXT

**Product type:** USCENTCOM J5 compilation of national and theater strategic guidance excerpts. **From:** USCENTCOM J5 (Plans), Strategy Division. **DTG of issue:** 150300Z MAR 2026. **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E).

> Abridged extracts from national strategy, Department of Defense direction, and theater strategy as they bear on northeast Syria. Extracts are compiled for planning use and are not the controlling documents.

## 1. National Security Strategy (relevant excerpts)

National strategy identifies the countering of Iranian malign influence across the region as an enduring objective, with specific reference to denying Iran a contiguous ground line of communication ("land bridge") from Iranian territory through Iraq and Syria toward the Mediterranean littoral. National strategy directs the prevention of the reconstitution of the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS) and the protection of gains achieved against it. National strategy emphasizes the use of partners and economy-of-force approaches, and the avoidance of large-scale, open-ended United States ground commitments.

## 2. National Defense Strategy (relevant direction)

Department strategy directs the imposition of cost on Iranian-backed consolidation in eastern Syria through partnered and irregular approaches, **without escalation to overt armed conflict** with the Syrian regime or with Iran. Department strategy designates the eastern Syria effort as a supporting effort within the broader theater campaign, to be resourced accordingly, and directs that the introduction of United States forces into ground combat in Syria is **not authorized** under this line of effort.

## 3. National Military Strategy / CJCS direction (relevant direction)

Military strategy directs combatant commands to develop options that achieve campaign objectives below the threshold of armed conflict, with explicit attention to authorities, attribution management, and escalation control. Support to vetted irregular forces conducting authorized unconventional warfare is to be conducted under Section 1202 (FY2018 National Defense Authorization Act); activities exceeding that authority are to be referred for separate authorization. All support to partner forces is conditioned on completion of human-rights (Leahy) vetting.

## 4. Theater strategy and commander's strategic estimate (excerpts)

The USCENTCOM theater strategy assesses the loss of partner-held terrain in the middle Euphrates valley as a reversal of counter-ISIS gains and as an enabler of Iranian land-bridge consolidation. The commander's strategic estimate assesses that an indigenous resistance — the Azadî Resistance Council (ARC) — retains latent capability in the Jazira but has been displaced and isolated from external resupply by recent Iranian-backed proxy action.

The theater strategy sets the desired conditions as: a viable indigenous capability able to coerce and disrupt regime and Iranian-backed proxy activity in the Jazira; a stalled consolidation in the middle Euphrates valley; and a minimized United States advisory footprint maintained below the threshold of overt armed conflict. The estimate notes that apportioned forces for the eastern Syria supporting effort are limited, that intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance and lift are shared theater assets prioritized against higher-priority requirements elsewhere in the area of responsibility, and that force closure for this line of effort is therefore time-phased and lift-dependent.

## 5. Authorities summary (planning-relevant)

- **Section 1202, FY2018 NDAA** — support to irregular forces, groups, or individuals conducting authorized unconventional warfare by United States special operations forces. Governing authority for this line of effort.

- **Title 10 / Title 50 boundary** — activities exceeding Section 1202, including activities properly characterized as covert action, require separate Title 50 or interagency authorization. The characterization of specific activities is a determination required of the command.

- **Leahy vetting** — provision of any assistance to partner units or individuals is conditioned on completion of human-rights vetting.

- **Absence of host-nation consent** — no status-of-forces or host-nation agreement covers United States activity in Syrian sovereign territory; the legal predicate rests on national authorities.

## 6. DIME integration

**Diplomatic.** Regional coordination is constrained: a North Atlantic Treaty Organization ally (Türkiye) opposes support to the ARC and denies northern access; host-nation consent is absent; cross-border access through Iraqi territory is tacit and politically reversible. Diplomatic effort is oriented on preserving Iraqi tolerance and managing allied friction.

**Informational.** The regime, Iranian-backed elements, and the Russian presence sustain a contested information environment in which United States and partner activity is subject to disinformation and pre-emptive attribution narratives. Information effort is oriented on exposing the Iranian and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps hand and countering disinformation, within deniability discipline.

**Military.** The military contribution is an unconventional-warfare supporting effort executed through SOCCENT, by, with, and through the ARC, below the threshold of overt armed conflict, and deconflicted with Russian forces present in theater.

**Economic.** Critical infrastructure in the operational environment — the Euphrates dams supplying power and water, and eastern hydrocarbon production — bears on the legitimacy and revenue of any actor controlling the valley and is a civil-vulnerability consideration.

## 7. Strategic risk framing

National and theater guidance accept the risk inherent in a partnered, deniable approach in a denied area, in exchange for avoiding a direct United States–regime or United States–Iran armed conflict. The strategic-risk emphasis is on attribution management, escalation control, and the human-rights legitimacy of the partner. Mission analysis admits no single right answer; the commander decides, grounded in doctrine.

## C2:D02 — Regional Intel Summary

UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E)

# 02 — REGIONAL INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY

**Product type:** USPACOM J2 higher-headquarters intelligence background. **From:** USPACOM **DTG of issue:** 150400Z MAR 2026. **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E).

> This summary provides the higher-headquarters intelligence background and escalation history for the mission-analysis staff. Assessments are stated at confidence; attribution of the Iranian directing hand is a cross-source assessment, not an open-source finding.

## 1. Strategic setting

Northeast Syria has remained a contested seam since the territorial defeat of the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS). The Syrian regime, lacking the regular manpower to administer the eastern governorates, has come to rely on Iranian-backed proxy manpower to extend control. Iran's enduring strategic objective in the area is the securing of a contiguous ground line of communication — the "land bridge" — from Iranian territory through Iraq and Syria toward the Mediterranean littoral. The middle Euphrates valley is the decisive terrain for that line of communication, and partner-held ground in the Jazira has been the principal obstacle to its completion.

## 2. Road to war — escalation arc (preceding 90 days)

- **D-90 to D-60.** Iranian-backed proxy militias (principally Liwa al-Sadiq) reinforce in the western and middle Euphrates valley under cover of regime "counter-terrorism" framing. Advisory activity attributed to an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps–Quds Force element (the Ansar Advisory Group) increases coordination among proxy formations.

- **D-60 to D-30.** Proxy forces conduct probing actions against Azadî Resistance Council (ARC) forward positions and interdict secondary supply movements along route BADGER. Regime intelligence activity against the ARC underground in Hasakah and Qamishli intensifies.

- **D-30 to D-7.** Proxy forces mass within indirect-fire range of the Tel Roj salient. Regional information channels pre-position an attribution narrative framing any escalation as a domestic counter-terrorism operation and accusing external powers of destabilization.

- **D-Day (150600Z MAR 2026).** Iranian-backed proxy forces seize the Tel Roj junction, sever route BADGER — the ARC's principal external support corridor — and displace the Roj Brigades from forward positions in the Tel Roj pocket, isolating the guerrilla force from external resupply.

## 3. Character of the offensive

The consolidation is led by Iranian-backed proxy militias — principally Liwa al-Sadiq, reinforced, with elements of Saraya Khorasan — operating alongside but distinct from Syrian regime regular formations. The proxy character of the lead echelon is assessed with moderate confidence and resolves only by cross-reading signals and human reporting against the adversary order of battle (document 03); surface and open-source reporting attribute the action to regime regulars. The Ansar Advisory Group is assessed to provide command, enabling, and indirect-fire coordination; its hand is concealed to preserve Iranian deniability. Employment of proxies in the lead is assessed as a deliberate measure to keep Iranian direction below the threshold of attributable state action.

## 4. Partner force — Azadî Resistance Council

The ARC is a Kurdish-led umbrella movement with Arab tribal affiliates of variable reliability. It is organized consistent with a doctrinal resistance:

- **Underground (Komel network).** Clandestine cells in the urban centers of Hasakah and Qamishli conducting intelligence, subversion, and early warning. Assessed penetrated by regime intelligence; communications security weak.

- **Auxiliary (Hêlîn network).** A clandestine support element providing logistics, safe houses, movement assistance, and early warning to the guerrilla force.

- **Guerrilla force (Roj Brigades).** The overt military arm, an estimated 2,500 fighters with limited heavy weapons, low ammunition stocks, and no organic sustainment independent of route BADGER.

Partner cohesion is assessed as adequate at present but degrading. Absent restoration of resupply, the Roj Brigades are assessed at risk of culmination or fragmentation within the near term — the binding planning timeline for any external support.

## 5. Population and terrain

The Jazira and the middle Euphrates valley present an ethnic-mosaic human terrain of Kurdish, Arab tribal, and minority communities. Partner legitimacy and freedom of movement vary by district: strong in Kurdish-majority districts of Hasakah and Qamishli, contested in Arab-majority districts along the Euphrates, where the ARC is viewed with suspicion and the population is more susceptible to regime and proxy influence. Internally displaced concentrations in Hasakah governorate add civil density and complicate the distinction between combatants and non-combatants.

## 6. Electronic and information environment

Adversary electronic warfare — attributed to proxy and Iranian-advisory systems and to Russian-enabled capability resident in theater — degrades Global Positioning System reception and tactical communications across the Euphrates valley. The information environment is contested by regime, Iranian, and Russian narratives, including a pre-emptive attribution narrative directed at United States and partner activity.

## 7. Residual ISIS threat

ISIS remnant cells persist in the Badia desert and exploit security vacuums. Diversion of regime and proxy attention toward a resistance buildup is assessed as a condition that could enable localized ISIS resurgence, with attendant risk to lines of communication and to the broader counter-ISIS gains the campaign seeks to protect.

## 8. External actors

- **Russia.** Maintains presence and air-defense coverage from Hmeimim air base; coverage extends over the Euphrates valley. A deconfliction mechanism exists; Russia is not a coordinating partner.

- **Türkiye.** Opposes the ARC, which Ankara assesses as linked to a designated terrorist organization, and denies cross-border access, overflight, and basing from the north.

- **Iraq.** Has not granted explicit consent for cross-border movement; tolerates low-visibility movement through the Rabia/Yaroubiya and al-Walid–al-Tanf area crossings, reversible under Iranian-aligned political pressure.

## 9. Assessment

The seizure of the Tel Roj junction is assessed as a deliberate, Iranian-enabled step to complete the land-bridge consolidation and to collapse the only viable indigenous resistance before it can be reinforced. The dominant unknowns are Iranian intent and the proxy reinforcement timeline, partner cohesion under pressure, and the trafficability of any restored or alternate corridor. The window in which external support can preserve the partner is assessed as narrow.

## 1. Command relationships (assessed)

Nominal command runs through the Syrian regime Eastern Region Command. In practice, the consolidation's maneuver is assessed to be directed by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps–Quds Force advisory element (Ansar Advisory Group) through the Iranian-backed proxy militias, with regime regulars providing a holding and administrative echelon. The arrangement preserves a regime "face" on the operation while Iranian direction is exercised through, and concealed behind, the proxies.

Regime regular formations are assessed as the holding and administrative echelon, dependent on proxy manpower for offensive reach. The Military Intelligence Directorate is assessed to maintain active penetration of the ARC underground and to run informant networks in the displaced-population centers.

## 3. Iranian-backed proxy militias

| **Element** | **Est. strength** | **Equipment** | **Role** | **Confidence** |
|:---|----|----|----|----|
| Liwa al-Sadiq (reinforced) | Reinforced brigade equivalent (~2,500–3,000) | Light infantry; technicals; 82mm/120mm mortars; multiple-rocket launchers; ATGM; man-portable air defense (limited) | Lead maneuver; seized and holds the Tel Roj junction | Moderate |

Proxy forces are assessed as proficient in dispersed light-infantry operations, indirect fire, and the interdiction of fixed routes and chokepoints. Sustainment flows from the Iraqi border along the consolidating ground line of communication. Proxy capability is assessed as sufficient to hold seized terrain and to interdict a restored corridor, but not to rapidly clear dispersed guerrillas across the wider Jazira without reinforcement.

## 4. Iranian advisory element

The **Ansar Advisory Group** (Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps–Quds Force) is assessed, with moderate confidence, to provide command and control, enabling functions (logistics coordination, indirect-fire integration, intelligence), and advisory presence to the proxy militias. Advisory nodes are assessed to be co-located with proxy assembly areas in the middle Euphrates valley. The advisory hand is deliberately concealed; its presence is inferred from intercept patterns, advisory signatures, and the proxies' improved coordination rather than from direct or open-source observation. An Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps casualty or captured advisor is assessed as the event most likely to force overt attribution and Iranian retaliation.

## 5. Electronic warfare and information operations

Proxy and Iranian-advisory systems, augmented by Russian-enabled capability resident in theater, are assessed to degrade Global Positioning System reception and tactical communications across the Euphrates valley, with effects concentrated along the corridor approaches. Regime, Iranian, and Russian information activity sustains a contested narrative environment and a pre-emptive attribution narrative framing the offensive as a domestic counter-terrorism operation.

## 6. Russian presence (context — not adversary, deconfliction only)

Russian Aerospace Forces operate from Hmeimim air base with air-defense coverage extending over the Euphrates valley. Russian forces are addressed through the deconfliction mechanism and are not assessed as a combatant against United States or partner forces; Russian air-defense posture nonetheless constrains United States air and aviation activity.

## 7. Assessment — capabilities versus intent

- **Capabilities (moderate-to-high confidence).** Sufficient to hold the Tel Roj junction, interdict fixed corridors with indirect fire, and contest a restored line of communication. No assessed capability to threaten the United States homeland or to conduct sustained operations beyond the valley without reinforcement.

- **Intent (low confidence — dominant unknown).** The depth and tempo of further consolidation and the proxy reinforcement timeline are not established. Iranian willingness to risk overt attribution is assessed as low; the proxy lead and concealed advisory hand are assessed as deliberate deniability measures.

- **Center-of-gravity framing (intelligence view).** The adversary source of strength is the Iranian-backed proxy force enabled by the advisory element; its critical requirement is the secured ground line of communication; its critical vulnerability is the deniability on which continued employment depends. Exposure of the Iranian hand is assessed to raise the political cost of continued consolidation.

## C2:D06 — Critical Geography

# 06 — CRITICAL GEOGRAPHY

**Product type:** USCENTCOM J2 (Geospatial) / J4 terrain and infrastructure analysis. **DTG of issue:** 150400Z MAR 2026. **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E).

> Terrain, lines of communication, chokepoints, infrastructure, and climatology bearing on infiltration, sustainment, and recovery. Place names are at open-source level; the named corridor (BADGER/SABER) and the Tel Roj pocket are fictional and exercise-only.

## 1. Operational area

The area of interest comprises the Jazira (Hasakah governorate) and the middle Euphrates valley (eastern Deir ez-Zor governorate), bounded by the Iraqi border to the east and southeast, Turkish territory to the north, and the Badia desert to the south. Key population centers are Hasakah, Qamishli, and Deir ez-Zor. Dominant natural features are the Euphrates and Khabur rivers and the Jebel Abdul Aziz ridge.

## 2. Observation, avenues, key terrain, obstacles, cover (OAKOC summary)

- **Observation and fields of fire.** Open desert and river-valley terrain afford long fields of observation along improved routes and crossing sites; broken ground near Jebel Abdul Aziz and urban terrain in Hasakah/Qamishli offer concealment for the underground and auxiliary.

- **Avenues of approach.** The eastern and southeastern border approaches (route BADGER, route SABER) are the feasible external avenues; the northern approach is closed by Turkish opposition.

- **Key terrain.** The Tel Roj junction (controls route BADGER); the Euphrates dams and hydrocarbon nodes (economic/legitimacy value); the border crossings (control external access).

- **Obstacles.** The Euphrates and Khabur rivers; mined and fortified chokepoints; adversary indirect-fire coverage of fixed routes; degraded position, navigation, and timing from electronic warfare.

- **Cover and concealment.** Urban terrain and the auxiliary network afford concealment; open desert on route SABER affords little.

## 3. Lines of communication

**Route BADGER (severed).** Principal external support corridor for the Tel Roj pocket: from the Iraqi border vicinity the Rabia/Yaroubiya crossing, southwest along secondary roads to the Tel Roj junction, into the pocket. Interdicted at the Tel Roj junction by Iranian-backed proxy action on 150600Z MAR 2026. The junction is the binding chokepoint; while held by hostile forces, the corridor is closed to sustained movement. Shortest route; lowest sustainment leg; highest fixed-chokepoint interdiction risk.

**Route SABER (alternate).** Longer alternate offsetting south through the desert margin from the al-Walid–al-Tanf area crossing, avoiding the Tel Roj junction but crossing open terrain under potential observation and within range of proxy indirect fire. Trades a fixed-chokepoint interdiction risk for exposure across distance and a longer, more lift-intensive sustainment leg.

**M4 highway.** Principal east–west paved artery; contested control; unsuitable as a clandestine support route.

## 4. Chokepoints and crossing sites

| **Node** | **Function** | **Status / risk** |
|:---|----|----|
| Tel Roj junction | Route BADGER chokepoint | Held by proxy forces; closed |
| Route SABER desert crossings | Alternate corridor crossing sites | Open terrain; observation/indirect-fire risk |
| Rabia/Yaroubiya crossing | Iraq border entry (BADGER) | Tacit Iraqi tolerance; reversible |
| al-Walid–al-Tanf area crossing | Iraq border entry (SABER) | Tacit Iraqi tolerance; reversible |

Trafficability of any corridor is contingent on the status of these nodes and on the tolerance of Iraqi authorities for movement through the border crossings.

## 5. Sustainment geography

No secure overland line of communication into the Tel Roj pocket exists following the severance of route BADGER. Sustainment of a force in the pocket must traverse distance from the Iraqi border under interdiction threat; throughput is capped by corridor capacity and by available lift, not by stockholding at the point of origin. The absence of northern access (Turkish opposition) removes the shortest potential line of support and forces reliance on the longer eastern and southeastern approaches. Aerial resupply and recovery transit Russian air-defense coverage and require deconfliction.

## 6. Critical infrastructure

- **Euphrates dams.** Regional power and water; control confers legitimacy and revenue; civil-vulnerability node.

- **Tellak gas field and eastern oil fields.** Hydrocarbon revenue; objectives of the consolidation.

- **M4 highway.** Principal east–west artery; contested.

## 7. Climatology and environment

March conditions: mild daytime temperatures, cold nights favorable to clandestine night movement; intermittent rain and periodic dust events reducing trafficability on unimproved desert tracks and degrading aerial observation and resupply. Reduced-illumination periods favor clandestine movement. Spring conditions are transitional; off-route surface movement is weather-dependent.

## 8. Geographic implications for planning

The corridor decision (route BADGER versus route SABER) is the decisive geographic problem and gates feasibility. The closure of the northern approach concentrates all external support on two contested eastern/southeastern corridors, each with distinct risk. Sustainment over distance, not stockholding, is the binding constraint, and all aerial activity is conditioned by Russian air-defense coverage and the deconfliction mechanism.

## 1. SITUATION

1.A. **General.** Iranian-backed proxy forces have conducted a consolidation offensive in the middle Euphrates valley, seized the Tel Roj junction, and severed route BADGER, the principal external support corridor to the indigenous resistance (Azadî Resistance Council), isolating the partner guerrilla force (Roj Brigades) from resupply. The action is assessed as a deliberate, Iranian-enabled step to complete the Iranian ground line of communication and to collapse the resistance before it can be reinforced.

1.B. **Enemy.** Iranian-backed proxy militias, advised by an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps–Quds Force element, leading the consolidation under regime cover, employing deniable proxy maneuver, indirect fire, electronic warfare, and information operations.

1.C. **Friendly.** The Azadî Resistance Council retains latent capability but is displaced and isolated. United States support is to be provided by, with, and through the partner. SOCCENT is the theater special-operations command for execution.

1.D. **Authorities.** Reference B (Section 1202) provides authority to support irregular forces conducting authorized unconventional warfare. No status-of-forces or host-nation agreement covers United States activity in Syrian sovereign territory; the legal predicate rests on national authorities per references A and B.

## 2. MISSION

When directed, USCENTCOM initiates unconventional-warfare support to the Azadî Resistance Council in eastern Syria, by, with, and through the partner and below the threshold of overt armed conflict, to disrupt Iranian-backed proxy consolidation of the middle Euphrates valley and preserve a viable partner capability, in order to deny completion of the Iranian ground line of communication.

## 3. EXECUTION

3.A. **Commander's intent (national).** The purpose is to deny Iranian land-bridge consolidation and preserve a viable indigenous capability in the Jazira at minimized United States exposure and below the threshold of overt armed conflict. The end state is an Azadî Resistance Council able to coerce and disrupt regime and Iranian-backed proxy activity, a stalled consolidation in the middle Euphrates valley, and a minimized United States advisory footprint. Acceptable risk is weighted toward preserving deniability and avoiding direct United States–regime or United States–Iran armed conflict.

3.B. **Concept (national).** A phased unconventional-warfare effort: prepare and vet the partner; re-establish a sustainable line of support; organize and enable the guerrilla force to disrupt the consolidation. The supporting effort is resourced as economy-of-force within the theater campaign.

3.C. **Tasks to USCENTCOM.**

- 3.C.1. Organize, advise, and support the Azadî Resistance Council, and prepare to execute unconventional-warfare support on order.

- 3.C.2. Conduct human-rights (Leahy) vetting of partner-force units and individuals prior to the provision of assistance.

- 3.C.3. Deconflict all operations with Russian forces in theater in accordance with reference E.

- 3.C.4. Plan and prepare to sustain the partner force; identify and report force, lift, and sustainment requirements and shortfalls.

- 3.C.5. Establish personnel-recovery arrangements for United States personnel operating in the denied area.

- 3.C.6. Submit the USCENTCOM mission analysis, commander's feasibility assessment, and nominated commander's critical information requirements prior to execution.

- 3.C.7. Coordinate with the Department of State and the interagency on access, attribution management, and the Title 10 / Title 50 boundary.

3.D. **Coordinating instructions.**

- 3.D.1. United States forces are not authorized to conduct ground combat operations in Syria. The United States role is limited to advise, assist, and enable, by, with, and through the partner.

- 3.D.2. Operations will not cross the threshold of overt United States–Syria armed conflict. Unilateral kinetic strikes against regime forces are not authorized absent separate national authorization.

- 3.D.3. All activity will be conducted within the scope of reference B. Activities exceeding that scope, including any activity properly characterized as covert action, will be referred for separate authorization.

- 3.D.4. Provision of assistance is conditioned on completion of Leahy vetting.

- 3.D.5. Do not employ basing, overflight, or access denied by Türkiye.

- 3.D.6. Maintain deniability discipline consistent with the below-threshold posture.

## 4. ADMINISTRATION AND LOGISTICS

4.A. Sustainment of partner forces is a USCENTCOM responsibility within apportioned resources; throughput is constrained by corridor capacity and shared theater lift. Report shortfalls. 4.B. The legal basis for activity rests on national authorities under references A and B; no host-nation agreement applies.

## 5. COMMAND AND SIGNAL

5.A. USCENTCOM is supported combatant command for this line of effort. USSOCOM provides forces as apportioned. SOCCENT exercises theater special-operations command and control. 5.B. Report mission analysis, feasibility assessment, commander's critical information requirements, and personnel-recovery readiness to the Joint Staff J3. 5.C. This order is effective for planning on receipt; execution on separate order.

## Title

Operation JAGGED ANVIL — JIPOE Brief. Area of responsibility: USCENTCOM. Joint operational area: the Jazira (Hasakah governorate) and the middle Euphrates valley (eastern Deir ez-Zor governorate), northeast Syria. Briefing date: 15 March 2026.

## Step 1 — Define the operational environment

**Joint operational area.** The Jazira and the middle Euphrates valley, bounded by the Iraqi border to the east and southeast, Turkish territory to the north, and the Badia desert to the south. Decisive movement nodes are the Tel Roj junction (route BADGER), the desert crossing sites of route SABER, and the Rabia/Yaroubiya and al-Walid–al-Tanf area border crossings. The Tel Roj pocket — the displaced enclave of the Azadî Resistance Council (ARC) — sits at the western end of route BADGER.

**Area of interest.** The wider corridor extending east into Iraq's western governorates (the proxy and Iranian logistics approach toward the border), north to the Turkish frontier (the constrained approach), and west along the Euphrates to the regime and Russian centers of gravity at and beyond Deir ez-Zor. The Iranian ground line of communication ("land bridge") transits this area of interest from the Iraqi border toward the western interior.

**Defining characteristics.** A denied operational environment inside Syrian sovereign territory, without host-nation consent, under partial Russian air-defense coverage; an active proxy consolidation that has severed the partner's only external support corridor; an ethnic-mosaic human terrain of variable permissiveness; a contested information environment; degraded position, navigation, and timing from adversary electronic warfare; and a residual ISIS threat in the desert margins. No overt United States–Syria armed conflict is in progress, and the central planning condition is the requirement to keep it that way while enabling the partner.

## Step 2 — Describe the impact of the operational environment (PMESII-PT)

- **Political (P).** The Syrian regime claims sovereignty over terrain it does not fully administer; the Eastern Region Command exercises regime authority in the area of interest. No status-of-forces or host-nation agreement covers United States activity; the legal predicate rests on national authorities. A North Atlantic Treaty Organization ally (Türkiye) opposes the ARC and denies northern access; Iraq tolerates only low-visibility cross-border movement, reversible under Iranian-aligned political pressure.

- **Military (M).** The lead echelon of the consolidation is Iranian-backed proxy militias (Liwa al-Sadiq, Saraya Khorasan), distinct from regime regulars, advised by an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps–Quds Force element (the Ansar Advisory Group). Regime regulars (9th Mechanized Brigade; a Republican Guard detachment) constitute the holding echelon. The ARC fields a guerrilla force (Roj Brigades, estimated 2,500) with an underground (Komel) and auxiliary (Hêlîn) network.

- **Economic (E).** The valley economy rests on hydrocarbon production (the Tellak gas field and eastern oil fields) and on the Euphrates dams supplying regional power and water; control of these nodes confers revenue and legitimacy on the controlling actor and is an object of the consolidation.

- **Social (S).** Kurdish, Arab tribal, and minority communities make partner legitimacy variable by district; Arab-majority districts along the Euphrates are less permissive to the Kurdish-led ARC. Internally displaced concentrations in Hasakah governorate add civil density and complicate combatant/non-combatant distinction.

- **Information (I).** Regime, Iranian, and Russian narratives contest the information environment, including a pre-emptive attribution narrative framing the fighting as a domestic counter-terrorism operation and accusing external powers of destabilization. Open-source reporting attributes the offensive to regime regulars, not to the proxy lead.

- **Infrastructure (I).** The Euphrates dams, hydrocarbon nodes, and the M4 highway are the critical infrastructure of the valley; all are contested and are civil-vulnerability considerations.

- **Physical environment (PE).** March: mild days, cold nights favorable to clandestine movement, intermittent rain and dust reducing trafficability on unimproved tracks and degrading aerial observation and resupply; a transitional spring pattern follows.

- **Time (T).** The binding timeline is the assessed risk that the Roj Brigades culminate or fragment before external support is restored. Corridor re-establishment is the pacing factor for any support.

## Step 3 — Evaluate the threat

Applying the capabilities-versus-intent distinction (Sherman Kent):

- **Capabilities (assessed with moderate-to-high confidence).** Proxy militias field light infantry, technical-mounted mobile elements, mortars and rocket artillery, and man-portable systems sufficient to hold seized terrain and interdict fixed corridors. Regime regulars provide armor and a holding capability. Adversary electronic warfare — proxy and Iranian-advisory systems augmented by Russian-enabled capability in theater — degrades the Global Positioning System and tactical communications across the valley. Regime intelligence and security services, including the Military Intelligence Directorate, are assessed to actively penetrate the ARC underground; partner counterintelligence is weak.

- **Intent (assessed with low confidence — the dominant unknown).** The depth and tempo of further consolidation, and the reinforcement timeline of proxy forces into the middle Euphrates valley, are not established. Iranian willingness to risk overt attribution of the directing hand is assessed as low; the proxy lead is assessed as a deliberate deniability measure, and the Ansar Advisory Group's hand is concealed. The proxy character of the lead echelon and the advisory presence resolve only through the cross-reading of signals and human reporting against the adversary order of battle (document 03), not from surface or open-source reporting.

- **Center-of-gravity framing (intelligence view, offered for staff use).** The adversary source of strength in the consolidation is assessed to be the Iranian-backed proxy force enabled by the advisory element; its critical requirement is the secured ground line of communication, and its critical vulnerability is the deniability on which continued employment depends. The staff will develop the operational implications.

## Step 4 — Threat courses of action (competing; not yet confirmed)

**Most likely.** Incremental consolidation along the valley: hold the Tel Roj junction to keep route BADGER closed; rely on proxy forces and indirect fire to interdict any restored corridor; preserve deniability and avoid actions that force overt attribution. *Indicators:* sustained proxy holding force at the junction; indirect-fire registration along corridor approaches; continued regime-attribution messaging; no surge of proxy reinforcement.

**Most dangerous.** Accelerated reinforcement of proxy forces into the middle Euphrates valley to collapse the Tel Roj pocket before external support is restored, accepting elevated attribution risk. *Indicators:* new proxy assembly areas and convoy flow from the Iraqi border; Ansar Advisory Group nodes forward; massing within indirect-fire range of the pocket; degradation of ARC cohesion through penetration or coercion.

**Branch — ISIS exploitation.** Diversion of regime and proxy attention toward the resistance enables localized ISIS resurgence from the Badia desert. *Indicators:* uptick in ISIS small-cell activity along the desert margins; attacks on lines of communication uncorrelated with the proxy offensive.

The relative ranking of the most-likely and most-dangerous courses turns on Iranian intent and the proxy reinforcement timeline, which are the dominant unknowns.

## Intelligence gaps (nominated for command attention)

- ARC cohesion and the extent of decisive regime-intelligence penetration through the buildup.

- Iranian and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps intent and the proxy reinforcement timeline.

- Trafficability and defensibility of route BADGER and route SABER for sustained resupply.

- Whether Russia will treat United States infiltration as a deconfliction matter or a provocation.

- Whether Iraqi authorities will tolerate or interdict cross-border sustainment.

## Named areas of interest (nominated)

- **NAI-1 / NAI-2** — Tel Roj junction (route BADGER chokepoint) and route SABER crossing sites: confirm trafficability and interdiction.

- **NAI-5** — proxy assembly areas and Ansar Advisory Group nodes in the middle Euphrates valley: confirm reinforcement and the advisory hand.

- **NAI-6** — Badia desert margins: confirm ISIS resurgence indicators.

- **NAI-7** — Hasakah/Qamishli urban: regime-intelligence activity against the underground.

- **NAI-8** — Euphrates valley airspace: Russian air-defense posture.

## Confidence assessment

High confidence in the physical, infrastructure, and human-terrain picture and in adversary capabilities. Low confidence in adversary intent, the proxy reinforcement timeline, partner cohesion and penetration, and corridor trafficability. The Iranian directing hand and the proxy lead are assessed at moderate confidence and rest on cross-source fusion, not on open-source or surface reporting.

## Implications for the mission-analysis staff

The staff should drive collection against the gaps above; treat corridor re-establishment as the decisive movement problem and assess route BADGER against route SABER; plan a combined partner command-and-control architecture resilient to electronic warfare; develop the auxiliary and underground networks for intelligence and clandestine logistics; account for shared, contested intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance that cannot be assumed persistent; plan information activity that exposes the Iranian and advisory hand within deniability discipline; and surface the partner-penetration, escalation, and time risks. The operational answers are the staff's to develop; mission analysis admits no single right answer.

E.2. **Assumption.** Continued functioning of the deconfliction mechanism is a planning assumption; its failure materially changes the feasibility of aerial infiltration, resupply, and recovery and would force reliance on ground means.

# 12 — COMMANDER'S INITIAL PLANNING GUIDANCE (CIPG)

**Product type:** Commander, USCENTCOM, initial planning guidance to the staff (per JP 5-0). **DTG of issue:** 150600Z MAR 2026. **Classification (synthetic):** UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E).

> Initial guidance to focus mission analysis and the operational design. This guidance frames the problem and sets planning priorities; it does not pre-decide the analysis. Mission analysis admits no single right answer — the commander decides, grounded in doctrine.

## 1. Operational design

**Operational environment / current state.** Iranian-backed proxy forces, advised by an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps element under regime cover, have seized the Tel Roj junction, severed route BADGER, and isolated the only viable indigenous resistance. United States forces are constrained from ground combat, lack host-nation consent, operate under Russian air-defense coverage, and must avoid overt armed conflict.

**Desired future state.** A vetted, organized, and sustained Azadî Resistance Council able to coerce and disrupt the consolidation; a stalled Iranian land-bridge consolidation; protected counter-ISIS gains; and a minimized United States footprint below the armed-conflict threshold.

**Problem statement.** The problem is to enable an indigenous partner to disrupt Iranian-backed consolidation of the middle Euphrates valley — by, with, and through the partner, within Section 1202 authority, without host-nation consent, and below the threshold of overt armed conflict — before the partner culminates.

**Operational approach (lines of effort).**

- LOE 1 — **Partner readiness:** vet, organize, and advise the Roj Brigades and develop the underground and auxiliary networks.

- LOE 2 — **Sustainable access:** re-establish a secure infiltration and resupply corridor; secure tacit Iraqi tolerance; establish personnel recovery.

- LOE 3 — **Disrupt consolidation:** enable partner action to coerce and disrupt the proxy line of communication, exposing the Iranian hand within deniability discipline.

- **Decisive condition:** reliable infiltration and sustainment of a vetted partner. Absent it, no approach is feasible.

## 2. Initial commander's intent

**Purpose:** deny completion of the Iranian ground line of communication and preserve a viable partner in the Jazira. **Method:** unconventional-warfare support, by, with, and through the Azadî Resistance Council, below the threshold of overt armed conflict. **End state:** the partner organized, vetted, and sustained to a viable guerrilla capability; consolidation disrupted; United States footprint minimized and below threshold. Weight risk acceptance toward preserving deniability and avoiding direct United States–regime or United States–Iran conflict.

## 3. Planning guidance by joint function

- **Command and control.** Establish a combined partner–task-force architecture resilient to electronic warfare; frame the by-with-through relationship clearly (the partner is supported, not commanded); fix the supported/supporting relationships with SOCCENT.

- **Intelligence.** Make priority requirements of partner reliability and penetration, adversary intent and the proxy reinforcement timeline, and corridor trafficability; treat ISIS resurgence as a monitored named area of interest; do not assume persistent intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance coverage.

- **Fires.** No unilateral strikes on regime forces; fires are partner-enabled and constrained by the below-threshold restraint; plan counter-indirect-fire as a movement and partner problem.

- **Movement and maneuver.** Treat corridor re-establishment as the decisive task; assess route BADGER against route SABER; plan infiltration consistent with Russian deconfliction.

- **Protection.** Personnel recovery for advisors is a precondition for infiltration; protect the advisory footprint and deniability; address partner penetration through vetting and operations security.

- **Sustainment.** Plan throughput against corridor capacity and lift, not stockholding; identify the binding sustainment shortfall and report it.

- **Information.** Expose the Iranian and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps hand and counter regime and Russian disinformation, within deniability discipline.

## 4. Constraints and restraints (commander's emphasis)

Must do: vet before assisting (Leahy); operate within Section 1202; deconflict with Russian forces. Cannot do: conduct United States ground combat; cross the threshold of overt armed conflict or strike regime forces unilaterally; use access Türkiye has denied.

## 5. Risk guidance

Treat as the priority risks: capture or loss of an advisor in the denied area (operational/strategic-recovery); regime-intelligence penetration of the partner (force); inadvertent escalation through disclosure or an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps casualty (escalation); and partner culmination before support arrives (time). Each is to be briefed with a mitigation. Surface the strategic risk of ISIS resurgence.

## 6. Planning priorities and CCIR nomination

Priorities, in order: (1) feasibility of infiltration and sustainment; (2) partner reliability and vetting; (3) the corridor decision; (4) personnel-recovery coverage; (5) deconfliction and cross-border access. Nominate commander's critical information requirements against partner cohesion, adversary intent and reinforcement, corridor status, Russian and Iraqi posture, and friendly readiness and recovery coverage. Identify the decision points the analysis must support, to include the commit-or-abort decision and the corridor-selection decision.

## 7. Guidance on the mission-analysis brief

Brief higher mission and intent, facts and assumptions, specified/implied/essential tasks, constraints and restraints, centers of gravity, risk, military objectives and evaluation criteria, commander's critical information requirements, the restated mission, and an initial force and sustainment assessment. Carry forward that there is no single right answer in mission analysis; present a sound, doctrinally grounded analysis and identify where commander's decisions are required.
