## C3:D01 — Strategic Context

# 01 — STRATEGIC CONTEXT — OPERATION CORAL TEMPEST

## National framing

The Southwest Pacific is a region of intensifying strategic competition conducted largely below the threshold of armed conflict. United States strategy in the region emphasizes the sovereignty and resilience of Pacific Island states, the primacy of regional partners, and the principle that assistance is offered in support of affected states rather than in pursuit of access. Disaster response has become a recognized arena of that competition: the state that delivers credible relief first, and is seen to respect the affected nation's sovereignty, accrues durable legitimacy and access.

## The affected state in the competition

The Republic of Vanuatu maintains relationships with the United States, Australia, New Zealand, and France, and has in recent years also accepted infrastructure investment and policing cooperation from the People's Republic of China. Vanuatu has historically balanced these relationships to preserve its own freedom of action. Vanuatu governance is presently fluid, and a parliamentary bloc favorable to closer alignment with the People's Republic of China is influential during the current period of political maneuvering.

## The competitor's approach

People's Republic of China relief and security elements were positioned to respond rapidly and to frame the People's Republic of China as the indispensable partner of the affected state. The competitor's advantage is presence and speed; its vulnerability is the perception, where it arises, that assistance is offered in pursuit of access rather than in the interest of the affected population. United States and partner advantage lies in scale, reach, and the legitimacy of a genuinely affected-state-led and multinational response.

## Allied and coalition framing

France, Australia, and New Zealand coordinate disaster response in the region through the FRANZ Arrangement, and each maintains standing relationships with Vanuatu. A United States contribution is expected to reinforce, not supplant, this regional architecture; a response that places Pacific and FRANZ partners forward carries greater legitimacy than a unilateral United States effort.

## Strategic guidance bearing on the operation

The intent at the national level is to relieve suffering, to reinforce the sovereignty and resilience of the affected state and the standing of its partners, and to deny the consolidation of competitor access — while keeping United States involvement below the threshold of armed conflict. Speed and legitimacy are to be pursued together. A response that is slow cedes the competition; a response that is heavy-handed or that proceeds without the affected state's consent undermines the very legitimacy it seeks to protect.

## C3:D02 — Regional Intel Summary

# 02 — REGIONAL INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY — OPERATION CORAL TEMPEST

## Disaster situation

Severe Tropical Cyclone RUA struck Efate at 050600Z FEB 2026 as a Category-5 system and tracked across the central islands. Initial reporting indicates severe damage to Port Vila, including to the Bauerfield International Airport airfield surfaces and terminal and to the Lapetasi (Port Vila) wharf. Power, water, and fixed communications across Efate are largely inoperative. Casualty and displacement figures are incomplete; displacement is reported across Efate, the Shepherd Islands, and parts of Espiritu Santo, with the most acute needs assessed in water, emergency medical care, and shelter.

## Political situation

Vanuatu governance is in a period of instability associated with a motion-of-no-confidence cycle. A parliamentary grouping favorable to the People's Republic of China — referred to in open reporting as the Vanua Tasi bloc — is maneuvering for advantage and is assessed to favor channeling international assistance through People's Republic of China auspices. Reporting indicates that access for United States and allied responders to Port Vila and to certain affected districts has been subject to restriction; whether this reflects a deliberate, directed policy or administrative confusion in the disaster's aftermath is not confirmed and is assessed at moderate confidence to be at least partly deliberate.

## Competitor presence

A People's Republic of China relief flotilla, including the People's Liberation Army Navy hospital ship and surface escorts and a China Coast Guard cutter, is operating in Vanuatu waters. People's Republic of China police advisors associated with the standing policing-cooperation arrangement are reported ashore. People's Republic of China messaging emphasizes the speed and generosity of its assistance and characterizes a United States military role as militarized and potentially destabilizing.

## Infrastructure and access

The damage to Bauerfield and to the Port Vila wharf has reduced the throughput of the principal air and sea points of entry. The Chinese-built wharf at Luganville on Espiritu Santo is intact and is a significant logistics node; its control and use are reported to be a point of competition. Outer-island populations are difficult to reach and are assessed to be underserved by the relief that has so far arrived.

## Assessment

The instigating event is a natural disaster, not an armed attack; the operational problem is shaped by contested access, a fluid host-nation political situation, and an active competitor seeking legitimacy and access under humanitarian cover. The surface characterization of the People's Republic of China presence as purely humanitarian should be distinguished from its assessed strategic-access purpose.

## C3:D05 — Country Fact Sheet

# 05 — COUNTRY FACT SHEET — REPUBLIC OF VANUATU — OPERATION CORAL TEMPEST

## Geography and population

The Republic of Vanuatu is a Y-shaped archipelago of some eighty islands in the Southwest Pacific. The population is concentrated on Efate (capital, Port Vila) and Espiritu Santo (Luganville), with substantial dispersed populations across the Shepherd Islands and the northern and southern island groups. The terrain is volcanic and mountainous, the population is largely coastal, and many communities are reachable only by sea or air.

## Government and politics

Vanuatu is a parliamentary republic. Governments are typically multi-party coalitions, and motions of no confidence are a frequent feature of political life. At the time of the cyclone, a motion-of-no-confidence cycle is in progress and no faction holds a settled majority. A parliamentary grouping referred to as the Vanua Tasi bloc favors closer alignment with the People's Republic of China and is influential in the current maneuvering; a caretaker leadership exercises authority pending resolution. The practical consequence for foreign responders is that the locus of host-nation consent is uncertain: requests and clearances routed through one set of officials may be countermanded or delayed by another.

## Foreign relations

Vanuatu maintains relationships with Australia, New Zealand, France, and the United States, and has accepted infrastructure investment and policing cooperation from the People's Republic of China. Vanuatu has a record of balancing external partners to preserve its own freedom of action, including declining arrangements perceived to constrain assistance from third parties.

## Society and humanitarian context

Vanuatu is among the most disaster-exposed states in the world and has prior experience hosting large international relief operations following major cyclones. Customary authority (chiefs) is significant at the community level, particularly on outer islands, and affects how relief is received and distributed. The United Nations humanitarian system, through the cluster mechanism, and a range of non-governmental organizations are established partners in Vanuatu disaster response.

## Implications for access

Implications for access Host-nation consent for foreign assistance is not assured and is presently disputed among Vanuatu authorities; access for United States and allied responders to Port Vila and to certain districts has been subject to restriction. Relief that depends on a single point of host-nation approval is therefore at risk, and access to dispersed outer-island populations is a distinct problem from access to the capital.

## C3:D06 — Critical Geography

UNCLASSIFIED // FOR EXERCISE / TRAINING USE ONLY (E)

# 06 — CRITICAL GEOGRAPHY — OPERATION CORAL TEMPEST

## Distance and reach

Vanuatu lies in the Southwest Pacific, distant from established United States support locations: Guam is approximately 4,000 nautical miles distant, Hawaii farther still, and continental staging farther again. French New Caledonia (Nouméa) lies approximately 500 kilometers to the southwest, and Australia's east coast roughly 1,800 kilometers to the west. The practical consequence is that the relief problem is bounded by lift and shipping over distance; the nearest substantial staging is allied (New Caledonia and Australia) rather than United States.

## Air points of entry

- **Bauerfield International Airport (Port Vila, Efate).** The principal international airport. Cyclone damage to airfield surfaces, navigational aids, and the terminal has reduced throughput; ramp space and materials-handling equipment are limited even when the field is usable. Restoration of throughput is a precondition for sustained fixed-wing relief into the capital.

- **Santo-Pekoa International Airport (Luganville, Espiritu Santo).** A secondary field, less affected, offering an alternate aerial entry point for the north.

## Sea points of entry

- **Lapetasi (Port Vila) wharf, Efate.** The principal commercial wharf, partially blocked and structurally degraded by the cyclone; berthing for relief shipping is constrained pending survey and clearance.

- **Luganville (Simon Peter) wharf, Espiritu Santo.** A large, modern, Chinese-built wharf, intact, capable of berthing large vessels. It is both a logistics opportunity and a point of competition, given its provenance and the competitor's interest.

- **Over-the-shore and sea-basing.** Given degraded fixed ports, delivery by landing craft, connectors, and air from sea-based shipping is a feasible alternative that reduces dependence on contested fixed infrastructure and on a single point of host-nation approval.

## Internal distribution

The dispersed, multi-island population means that arrival of relief at a port of entry does not equate to delivery; distribution to the Shepherd Islands and other outer groups requires rotary-wing and small-craft movement and is the harder half of the throughput problem. Customary authority and damaged local infrastructure further shape distribution.

## Environmental factors

The post-cyclone environment features degraded fixed communications, intermittent positioning, navigation, and timing reliability, debris-fouled sea and air approaches, and the continuing cyclone season. These factors compound the distance problem and reduce the assumption of persistent situational awareness.

# 10 — JIPOE BRIEF — OPERATION CORAL TEMPEST

## Step 1 — Define the operational environment

The operational environment is the Republic of Vanuatu and its approaches in the Southwest Pacific, set within a region of strategic competition below the threshold of armed conflict. Relevant actors are the affected state (with a contested internal politics), the United States, the FRANZ partners (France, Australia, New Zealand), the United Nations humanitarian system and non-governmental organizations, and the People's Republic of China as a competitor present in the disaster space.

## Step 2 — Describe environmental effects

- **Physical.** A Category-5 cyclone has degraded the principal air and sea ports of entry on Efate, disrupted communications and positioning/navigation/timing, and complicated movement to dispersed outer islands. Distance from United States support locations bounds the relief effort; the nearest substantial staging is allied (New Caledonia, Australia).

- **Civil.** Mass displacement and acute needs in water, medical care, and shelter; a multi-island population reached only by air and sea; customary authority significant at the community level; an established United Nations cluster system and non-governmental presence.

- **Political/information.** Host-nation consent is contested among Vanuatu authorities. The information environment is actively contested: People's Republic of China messaging frames its assistance as benevolent and a United States military role as militarized.

## Step 3 — Evaluate the threat / competitor

The People's Republic of China presence comprises a naval hospital ship and escorts, a coast-guard cutter, police advisors ashore, and infrastructure leverage (the Luganville wharf). People's Republic of China activity is gray-zone and below the threshold of armed conflict; force is assessed unlikely, while shadowing, visibility competition, and exploitation of United States or allied missteps are likely. The surface presentation of the presence as humanitarian is distinguished from its assessed strategic-access purpose. Regime-style penetration is not the issue here; the competitor's strength is narrative and presence, and its vulnerability is the perception of self-interest and coercion.

## Step 4 — Determine threat/competitor courses of action and analytic gaps

The most likely competitor course of action is to maintain a visible, "benevolent" relief presence, deepen access through a sympathetic host-nation bloc, and avoid any incident that would justify a firm United States or allied response. A course of action of concern is the use of the host-nation bloc to restrict United States and allied access while the People's Republic of China fills the resulting gap. Key gaps the staff must resolve through information requirements include the true locus and stability of host-nation consent, People's Republic of China intent and likely response to a United States and allied effort, the throughput and restoration timeline of the ports of entry, and the location and needs of dispersed populations. This brief does not nominate information requirements, decision points, or courses of action for the friendly force; those are products of the mission analysis.

## Annex A — Foreign humanitarian assistance authorities (extract)

United States foreign disaster assistance is led by the United States Agency for International Development, Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance. Department of Defense provides support, governed by the following standing limits:

- Support is **request-driven** — provided in response to a validated request for assistance and a mission tasking, not undertaken unilaterally.

- Support is limited to capabilities **not reasonably available** from civilian, commercial, host-nation, or other international sources (the "unique capability" or "last-resort" test).

- Support is **time-bound** and intended to bridge to civilian and host-nation capacity, with a defined transition.

- Support is **cost-reimbursable** under the applicable authorities and is accounted accordingly.

- Immediate-response authority by an on-scene commander to save lives or prevent suffering is narrowly bounded and does not substitute for the request-and-tasking process.

## Annex B — Civil-military coordination and humanitarian principles (extract)

Relief is conducted consistent with humanitarian principles (humanity, neutrality, impartiality, independence). Military forces provide requested assistance and support to the humanitarian response. Coordination with the United Nations humanitarian system occurs through the cluster mechanism and an on-scene humanitarian-military coordination element. Direct military delivery of assistance is a last resort where civilian means are insufficient; the use of military assets is intended to preserve, not blur, the distinction between humanitarian and military action.

## Annex C — Rules of engagement / rules for the use of force (extract)

The operation is conducted below the threshold of armed conflict. Force is authorized only for self-defense and the defense of designated persons and units. Engagement, harassment, or provocation of People's Republic of China forces is not authorized; the standing requirement is to deconflict, to avoid incidents, and to report contact. Actions on Vanuatu sovereign territory require host-nation consent or appropriate tasking. Nothing in these rules is to be construed as authorizing an armed challenge to competitor forces or an entry without consent.

## Annex D — Coordination references

FRANZ Arrangement (France–Australia–New Zealand) for regional disaster-response coordination; the United Nations cluster system (water/sanitation/hygiene, health, shelter, logistics, protection); host-nation national disaster management arrangements. These annexes provide reference detail only; specified and implied tasks, information requirements, and decision points are developed in the mission analysis.

# COMMANDER'S INITIAL PLANNING GUIDANCE (CIPG) — OPERATION CORAL TEMPEST

**From:** Commander, United States Indo-Pacific Command **To:** USINDOPACOM Staff; Commander, Joint Task Force CORAL (on establishment) **Subject:** Initial planning guidance for mission analysis — FHA/HADR, Republic of Vanuatu **Reference:** CJCS Execute Order, Operation CORAL TEMPEST; JP 3-29; JP 5-0

## 1. Initial understanding of the problem

A catastrophic cyclone has struck a partner archipelago at a moment of internal political uncertainty, and a competitor is already present and visibly providing relief. The command's task is to provide foreign disaster relief quickly and credibly, by, with, and through the FRANZ partners, without crossing the threshold into an armed incident and without displacing the civilian and host-nation lead. The hard problems are problems of access, legitimacy, and reach — not of combat.

## 2. Commander's intent (initial)

The relief effort is to be fast, visibly cooperative with the affected state and the FRANZ partners, and disciplined in keeping the United States military in a support and assist role. Speed and legitimacy are to be weighed together; neither a slow response nor an overbearing one serves the end state. The command's standing is best protected by enabling others and by being seen to respect Vanuatu sovereignty.

## 3. Planning priorities (in order)

The staff is directed to prioritize the analysis as follows:

1.  Feasibility of relief delivery given degraded ports and the distances involved.

2.  Host-nation consent and legitimacy, and the civilian (USAID) and host-nation lead.

3.  Coordination and integration with the FRANZ partners and the United Nations humanitarian system.

4.  Escalation management and deconfliction with co-located competitor forces.

5.  Force protection and the preservation of humanitarian neutrality.

## 4. Guidance on tempo and risk

Time is a weighted factor: human suffering and the competition for legitimacy both reward speed, but not at the cost of an incident or a sovereignty violation. The staff is to surface where speed and prudence are in tension and to frame those tensions for decision rather than resolving them by assumption.

## 5. Planning constraints and boundaries

- DOS and USAID are the lead agency. INDOPACOM offers the lead agency capabilities not reasonably available from civilian or host-nation sources.

- No activity is to be planned on Vanuatu sovereign territory that presumes consent not yet granted.

- Nothing in the relief effort is to be planned in a manner that could be construed as an armed challenge to competitor forces.

## 6. Direction on analytic products

The staff is to conduct a full mission analysis in accordance with JP 5-0 and to brief, at minimum: higher mission and intent; facts and assumptions; specified, implied, and essential tasks; constraints and restraints; centers of gravity; risk; military objectives and evaluation criteria; the commander's critical information requirements; the restated mission; and an initial force and sustainment assessment. **The commander's critical information requirements and the decision points the analysis must support are to be derived and nominated by the staff; none are pre-specified in this guidance.** Carry forward that there is no single right answer in mission analysis; present a sound, doctrinally grounded analysis and identify clearly where commander's decisions are required.

## J1 — Personnel

Joint manning for a joint task force headquarters can be sourced but closes over time from distance. Individual augmentation for civil-military coordination, contracting, and liaison to the FRANZ partners and the United Nations is a limiting factor for the coordination architecture.

## J2 — Intelligence

Intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance is a shared theater resource in competition with other priorities; persistent coverage of Vanuatu and its approaches cannot be assumed. Post-cyclone degradation of communications and positioning/navigation/timing further limits situational awareness. Collection against host-nation political dynamics and competitor intent depends heavily on diplomatic and open-source reporting.

## J3 — Operations

Apportioned forces are limited: a joint task force headquarters, an amphibious ready group with embarked Marine air-ground elements, intra-theater airlift, and engineer, medical, and logistics detachments. Sea-basing and over-the-shore delivery are feasible and reduce dependence on degraded fixed ports.

## J4 — Logistics

Logistics is the binding factor. The problem is throughput over distance, not stockholding: relief must be moved across thousands of nautical miles and then distributed among dispersed islands. Degraded ports at Port Vila constrain fixed-port throughput; the intact Luganville wharf and sea-basing are the principal alternatives. Lift and sealift are shared, time-phased assets; closure of the full force is measured in weeks, while the most acute needs are measured in days.

## J5 — Plans

The relief problem is bounded by access (host consent and port status), reach (lift and shipping over distance), and legitimacy (the affected-state-led, multinational character of the response). The staff assesses that the decisive constraints are the contested consent and the throughput over distance.

## J6 — Communications

Fixed communications on Efate are largely inoperative; deployable and satellite communications are required from the outset and are themselves lift-dependent. A communications architecture resilient to the degraded environment is a precondition for coordinated relief.

## Surgeon / Medical

Acute needs are assessed in trauma care, water and sanitation, and disease prevention among displaced populations. Afloat medical capability (sea-based) offsets the degraded fixed medical infrastructure ashore and the presence of a competitor hospital ship in the same space.
